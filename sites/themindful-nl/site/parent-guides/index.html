For parents · ages 5–12

When your child gets stuck in big feelings

No quick labels. Just warm, practical first steps for meltdowns, worry, fear of failure, overwhelm and low confidence.

Find what may help nowTalk through your question

Important: The Mindful Kids teaches preventive skills; it does not diagnose or provide treatment. If difficulties persist, fear disrupts daily life, sleep stays poor for weeks, or you are worried, contact your GP, youth doctor or local support team. In immediate danger, call your GP or out-of-hours medical service.

Start with what you see

Choose the situation that feels familiar

01

My child has big emotions or meltdowns. What helps first?

Do not begin with explanations while their body is still on high alert. Stay nearby, lower your voice and reduce stimulation. Briefly name what you see: ‘This feels like a lot.’ Talk about what happened and one helpful next step only after your child is calm.

  • ✓Make the moment safe and quiet
  • ✓Slow your own breathing
  • ✓Name without judging
  • ✓Reflect after calm returns
Read: when big feelings come →
02

My child falls apart after school. Could it be overwhelm?

Some children hold everything together at school and release it at home. Build a transition first: few questions, a drink, a predictable quiet place and connection later. Track which days, sensory demands and transitions feel hardest for one week.

  • ✓Ten minutes without questions
  • ✓Water and a snack
  • ✓Quiet or rhythmic movement
  • ✓One open question later
Read the after-school decompression guide →
03

My child worries or feels afraid a lot. How should I respond?

Take the fear seriously without confirming that the situation is dangerous. Ask what your child expects might happen, choose one manageable step together and praise the attempt. Long-term avoidance can strengthen fear; gentle supported practice is often more useful.

  • ✓Listen and reflect
  • ✓Ask what feels scary
  • ✓Choose one small practice step
  • ✓Praise courage, not perfection
Practise calm breathing together →
04

My child fears failure and refuses to start.

Move attention away from the result and towards trying. Make the task smaller, name what is already working and let mistakes look normal. Try: ‘We do not need to know it yet; we are only trying step one.’

  • ✓Make step one tiny
  • ✓Use the word ‘yet’
  • ✓Praise strategy and effort
  • ✓Pause before exhaustion
See our method for courage and recovery →
05

My child is sensitive, shy or easily overwhelmed.

Sensitivity does not need to be trained away. Preparation, two clear choices and a safe way to pause can help. Practise new situations in small doses and tell the teacher what makes participation easier for your child.

  • ✓Explain what will happen
  • ✓Offer two clear choices
  • ✓Plan recovery time
  • ✓Share helpful signals with school
Find a small, playful group →
06

My child lacks confidence. What can I do every day?

Confidence grows from lived experiences: trying something, feeling supported and discovering that recovery is possible. Give specific feedback—‘you kept trying’—and offer small, genuine responsibilities.

  • ✓Give specific praise
  • ✓Let your child make choices
  • ✓Make progress visible
  • ✓Share your own learning moments
